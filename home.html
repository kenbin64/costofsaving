Face value, and actual value

Every calculator here does the same job: it takes the figure you were shown and works out the one you will live with.

The one question: does anything depend on it?

Sometimes face value is right. Whether it is comes down to what relies on the thing you are cutting, adding, buying or putting off.

Nothing depends on it It is a plain sum.

Cancel a subscription nobody uses and you save exactly what it cost. Add a fee nobody minds and you gain exactly the fee. Add or subtract, and the figure you were shown is the figure you get.

Something depends on it It is no longer a plain sum.

Cut what other things were relying on, and they go with it. Each one passes on only what it received, so the loss multiplies. Put something off, and what it was holding back keeps growing.

A hidden cost can show up at once or months later. It can land somewhere that looks unrelated. Often it has no line of its own. It is still in the mix, and the ones without a line can matter most.

Finding out which of the two you are looking at, and what the second one costs, is what every calculator here is for. Why it multiplies

For hiring teams

How many interview rounds is the right number?

Each round lowers the risk of a hire that does not work out. Each one also takes interviewers' time and another week with the seat open, while candidates with other offers accept elsewhere. See which round stops paying for itself, and keep your first choice.

The early rounds catch the most. The later ones have less left to catch, and cost nearly as much.
