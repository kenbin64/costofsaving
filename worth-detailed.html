Is it worth it? In detail

Warranties, subscriptions, big purchases, pawn loans, a degree. Pick what you are deciding, start from a common example or your own numbers, and see the one figure that settles it.

What are you deciding?

What kind of deal?

The price for one go: one policy term, one ticket, one spin. "How many times" repeats it: 52 weekly tickets, 600 spins.

Average payback vs what you pay, per go

Where each outcome sits

Across: the chance. Up: the payout. The black line joins every single outcome that would pay back exactly the price (chance × payout = price). A one-outcome deal above the line comes out ahead on average. With several outcomes, their payback adds up, which the bar above shows.

The two numbers behind every deal

chance × payout = average payback

Multiply to gather the deal into one number: what it returns on average each time. Several outcomes? Add their products.

price ÷ payout = break-even chance

Divide to split it back apart: how likely the outcome would have to be for the price to be fair.

times × (price − payback) = expected loss

Repeat a losing deal and the loss grows with every go. That is why an hour of slots costs far more than one spin.

A rule that holds up
  • If you could pay the loss yourself, skip protection that loses money on average. Keep the price instead.
  • If the loss would wreck you (a house, a big medical bill), paying a bit over the average can be the right call. The extra buys you out of a loss you could not absorb.
  • Bets that lose on average are entertainment. Set the budget as the ticket price and stop there.
What this cannot tell you
  • Your real chance. The answer is only as good as that number. Look for repair or claim rates for the exact product.
  • Fine print: claim denials, deductibles, and cover you may already have through a credit card or employer.
  • Taxes on prizes, and how much you enjoy playing.
