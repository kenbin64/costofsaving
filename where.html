Two bottom lines

On-site, hybrid or work from home?

Where the work happens changes two bottom lines at once: the business's, and each person's. Put in your team and see both.

This page does not recommend an arrangement. The right one depends on the work, the people and the place, and the same sums give different answers for different teams. The three examples below each come out a different way.

      What each arrangement is weighed for

      These are the points most often raised for each, by the people who favour it and the people who do not. They are considerations, not findings. Which of them apply to your team, and how strongly, is for you to put in the figures above.

      On-site

      Often raised for it

      • New people learn faster beside experienced ones.
      • Questions get answered in passing, without a meeting.
      • Shared equipment, secure systems and physical work are in one place.
      • Home and work stay separate.

      Often raised against it

      • Desks and buildings are a large fixed cost.
      • The commute costs each person money and hours.
      • Hiring is limited to people within reach of the office.

      Hybrid

      Often raised for it

      • Days together for the work that needs them, days apart for the work that does not.
      • Fewer desks when they are shared.
      • Less commuting without losing the office.

      Often raised against it

      • It needs co-ordinating: the days in only help if the right people are in.
      • Shared desks do not suit every kind of work.
      • The office is still paid for on the days it is half empty.

      Work from home

      Often raised for it

      • Little or no office cost.
      • No commute for the people doing the work.
      • Hiring is not limited by distance.
      • Long stretches of uninterrupted time.

      Often raised against it

      • New people can take longer to get up to speed.
      • Working together takes more planning.
      • Equipment, home costs and occasional travel replace some of the saving.
      • Not every home suits working in, and not every job can be done from one.

      The evidence on output and on who leaves is mixed and differs by kind of work. That is why this page supplies no figure for either and asks for yours.

      What this cannot tell you

      • How an arrangement changes output. That differs from team to team and is argued over. The page takes your figure and shows how far it can be wrong before the answer changes.
      • How many people an arrangement will drive away or draw in. That is your estimate too.
      • Work that can only be done in one place, and what the law or a contract requires.
      • What is not money: how well people work together, how new people learn, and what each person's home allows.
